About the Dashboard

The Disaster Risk and Vulnerability Dashboard is an interactive decision-support tool designed to help government planners, disaster risk reduction offices, and policy-makers identify areas that may require priority intervention across the Philippines.

The dashboard combines multiple indicators related to:

  • hazard exposure
  • poverty incidence
  • coping capacity
  • disaster vulnerability
  • typhoon activity

through synchronized maps, comparison panels, and bivariate analysis visualizations.

Unlike static reports, this interactive platform allows users to:

  • explore geographic patterns dynamically
  • compare provinces, regions, or island groups
  • identify overlapping vulnerabilities
  • examine how poverty, hazard exposure, and capacity interact
  • support evidence-based prioritization and resource allocation

Dashboard Structure

The dashboard follows a guided analytical workflow:

  1. Hazard — Explores areas with higher disaster and typhoon exposure.
  2. Capacity — Examines coping capacity and resilience indicators across locations.
  3. Poverty — Highlights economically vulnerable populations.
  4. Overlap — Combines indicators using bivariate analysis to identify compounded vulnerabilities and structural risk patterns.
  5. Priority — Summarizes high-priority areas that may require targeted intervention, preparedness planning, or resource allocation.

Interactive Features

Users can:

  • filter by geographic level
  • compare island groups, regions, or provinces
  • toggle typhoon track overlays
  • filter storms by intensity
  • interact with synchronized maps and scatterplots
  • explore relative position from national medians

The bivariate analysis dynamically updates depending on the selected comparison mode, helping users understand both spatial and statistical relationships between indicators.

Intended Use

This dashboard is intended to support:

  • disaster preparedness planning
  • vulnerability assessment
  • prioritization of interventions
  • policy analysis
  • regional comparison
  • exploratory spatial analysis

The platform is designed as an analytical aid and should complement official planning frameworks, field assessments, and institutional decision-making processes.

Data Sources

Data integrated into the dashboard may include:

  • Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA)
  • Pacific Disaster Center (PDC)
  • PAGASA tropical cyclone tracks
  • resilience and coping capacity indicators
  • administrative boundary datasets

Interpretation Guide

The dashboard is designed to support exploratory analysis and prioritization by identifying geographic patterns, overlapping vulnerabilities, and relative differences across provinces, regions, and island groups.

The visualizations and statistical relationships shown in this platform should be interpreted as:

  • pattern-based observations
  • relative comparisons
  • spatial tendencies
  • vulnerability clustering

rather than direct evidence of causal relationships.

Important Analytical Considerations

Correlation Does Not Imply Causation

The bivariate analysis and inferential statistics presented in the dashboard are intended to identify associations and patterns between variables such as:

  • poverty incidence
  • hazard exposure
  • coping capacity
  • disaster vulnerability

These relationships do not demonstrate that one variable directly causes another.

For example: a province with high poverty and high hazard exposure may show compounded vulnerability, but the dashboard alone cannot determine whether poverty causes disaster vulnerability or whether other structural factors influence both conditions.

Purpose of the Dashboard

The dashboard is intended to:

  • guide prioritization
  • support exploratory policy analysis
  • identify areas requiring further assessment
  • highlight potential vulnerability hotspots
  • assist resource allocation discussions

It should not be used as the sole basis for causal conclusions or final intervention decisions.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting results:

  • Indicators are aggregated at administrative levels and may mask local variation.
  • Provincial or regional averages may not fully represent conditions at the community level.
  • Some datasets may differ in collection year, methodology, or spatial resolution.
  • Bivariate classifications simplify complex social and environmental relationships into broad analytical categories.
  • Typhoon overlays represent historical spatial exposure and not deterministic future outcomes.

Recommended Complementary Approaches

To better understand causal drivers and local conditions, the dashboard should be complemented with:

  • field validation
  • barangay-level assessments
  • household-level surveys
  • community consultations
  • vulnerability and needs assessments
  • qualitative contextual analysis
  • longitudinal and causal research methods

Examples of stronger causal approaches include:

  • panel or longitudinal studies
  • quasi-experimental methods
  • spatial econometric modelling
  • household vulnerability analysis
  • localized resilience assessments

Granular Decision-Making

While the dashboard identifies broader geographic patterns, effective disaster planning often requires more granular analysis at:

  • barangay level
  • municipal level
  • household level
  • community level

Localized assessments are important because vulnerability can vary significantly even within the same province or region.

Intended Interpretation

The dashboard should therefore be viewed as a strategic screening and prioritization tool, not a definitive causal model.

Its primary value lies in helping planners recognize emerging patterns, compare relative vulnerability, identify overlap areas, and guide deeper investigation and targeted intervention planning.

        Decision note

        Priority logic

        Vulnerability index × Capacity gap = Priority score

        Vulnerability is the average of normalized poverty and hazard exposure. Capacity gap is computed as 1 minus normalized coping capacity. Higher priority scores indicate greater need for intervention.

        Data notes: All indicators are normalized from 0 to 1 for comparability across provinces. The priority score supports decision-making and should not be interpreted as causal evidence.
